# Chapter 12 — We Do Not Experience Sense as Absent

There is a temptation to imagine sense-making as if it begins from nothing.

First there is confusion.

Then we search.

Then something makes sense.

The picture seems natural because the moment of realization can feel dramatic. Before it, we did not have the answer. After it, we do.

But that picture hides something more basic.

To be confused, something must already be there to be confused about.

To search, something must already matter enough to be sought.

To notice a failure, something must already count as not working.

To ask a question, enough must already be organized for the question to have a shape.

So the stronger claim is not that sense appears out of its absence.

It is this:

> **We may never experience sense itself as absent. We experience its limits.**

Sense-making happens at those limits.

## There is no experiential zero

Try to imagine a condition in which nothing has yet become intelligible enough for anything to follow.

Not darkness.

Darkness is already something that can be distinguished from light.

Not noise.

Noise is already organized enough to be heard as noise.

Not confusion.

Confusion is already a relation between what is available and what is not yet enough.

Not even blankness.

Blankness is already a condition we can identify.

The moment a condition can appear as a condition, some difference has become available.

This does not prove that there is no metaphysical state outside sense.

It makes a narrower and more important claim.

> **There is no experiential position from which we can observe sense beginning from absolute zero.**

Every identifiable opening is already structured enough to be an opening.

Every question begins too late.

## An opening is not nothing

This matters for the architecture developed so far.

Making Sense Together begins from an open matter.

But an open matter is not an empty field.

A door is locked.

A diagnosis is uncertain.

A route is blocked.

A sentence is unclear.

A relation has not yet been established.

In every case, enormous amounts are already settled.

There is a door.

There is an aim.

There is a distinction between open and closed.

There is some reason the difference matters.

There is a field in which the present condition can count as insufficient.

The unresolved part exists inside an already intelligible situation.

That means insufficiency is not the opposite of sense.

> **Insufficiency is a distinction made possible by sense.**

Something can be not enough only because enough is already available around it to make the lack definite.

## Sense-seeking is not sense-making

This helps separate two activities that are easily collapsed.

**Sense-seeking** belongs to the opening.

Something is not enough.

We look.

We compare.

We remember.

We ask.

We move.

We test.

We imagine.

We try another route.

All of that may alter the conditions of the matter.

But none of it guarantees that the matter will become enough.

Sense-making names the achievement.

Something changes.

The change becomes sufficient for continuation.

Something from that achievement remains available.

The distinction matters because seeking can occur without making.

A person can search and fail.

They can search in the wrong place.

They can gather more information without reaching a completion.

And the reverse can also happen.

Sense can be made without deliberate seeking.

A relation suddenly becomes visible.

A chance remark reorganizes a problem.

The world changes and removes the obstruction.

A distinction becomes apparent before anyone intentionally looked for it.

So sense-making cannot be reduced to the conscious activity of a seeker.

Seeking is one way an open matter is worked.

Making is the event by which it becomes enough.

## The click

Ordinary language already has a good word for the transition.

Something **clicks**.

Before the click, the relevant materials may already be present.

The words have been read.

The diagram has been inspected.

The examples have been considered.

The problem has been turned over repeatedly.

Then something changes.

A relation becomes operative.

A distinction becomes usable.

A possibility opens.

A fault is located.

Separate parts become one organization.

A matter settles.

And almost immediately, attention moves through the achievement toward what it now makes possible.

That is why the event is difficult to inspect.

Before it, we are seeking.

After it, we are continuing.

The click itself is a disappearing middle.

> **Successful sense-making converts itself almost immediately into a new starting point.**

The achievement recedes into use.

## Inheritance is on both sides of the event

The earlier account of inheritance can now be deepened.

At first, inheritance appears to come after completion.

Something becomes enough.

Something remains.

What remains changes what can happen next.

But if every opening is already structured, inheritance is also there before the event.

What remains from earlier achievements helps form the field in which a new insufficiency can appear.

> **It produces future conditions of sense-making.**

## The present is not empty either

This gives another way to describe the present.

The present is not simply an instant between past and future.

It is full of things already worked out.

Words.

Habits.

Expectations.

Skills.

Distances.

Objects.

Relations.

Promises.

Rules.

Tools.

Ways of seeing.

Most of these do not appear as memories.

They appear as the ordinary structure of the situation.

The past therefore enters the present through more than recollection.

It enters through organization.

Some of that organization is personal.

Some is cultural.

Some is material.

Some is linguistic.

Some is institutional.

Some is embodied.

The present is already thick with inheritance.

And within that inheritance, some matter becomes live.

Something no longer holds.

Something is missing.

Something becomes possible.

Something becomes questionable.

Attention gathers there.

This suggests a useful formulation:

> **The present is the moving interface between what we can continue from and what we must work out anew.**

## Attention may be a reopening mechanism

Attention often moves toward the places where inherited organization has ceased to be sufficient.

A sound differs from expectation.

A familiar route is blocked.

A sentence does not fit.

A person's reaction is surprising.

A machine behaves differently.

A remembered rule fails in a new case.

Something that had been background becomes live.

This does not mean attention is only for failure.

Novel possibility can also reopen a field.

Something previously unavailable becomes reachable.

A distinction becomes newly consequential.

A question appears that could not previously be asked.

But in both cases, attention is drawn toward a limit in what can simply be continued from.

So another hypothesis becomes available:

> **Attention is one of the ways inherited sense is selectively reopened.**

What had been allowed to remain implicit becomes explicit enough to work on again.

## We experience limits, not the absence of sense

This is the central claim.

We can experience not knowing.

We can experience ambiguity.

We can experience disorientation.

We can experience contradiction.

We can experience shock.

We can experience a breakdown in what previously held.

But each of these is already structured.

Not knowing is not nothing.

It is knowing enough for a missing answer to matter.

Ambiguity is not nothing.

It is enough organization for more than one live possibility to remain.

Disorientation is not nothing.

It is a failed relation between inherited orientation and the present field.

Contradiction is not nothing.

It requires claims structured enough to conflict.

Even radical confusion is still a mode in which something is available as not yet enough.

So the boundary is not between sense and absolute non-sense.

It is a boundary inside intelligibility itself.

> **Sense-making happens where achieved intelligibility encounters its own local limit.**

## This changes what sense-making is

If this is right, sense-making is not best pictured as manufacturing meaning out of absence.

It is better pictured as reorganization.

An already intelligible inheritance reaches a point where it is no longer sufficient for continuation.

Something changes in the relation between the system and its situation.

A new organization becomes enough.

That organization then joins the inheritance from which later events begin.

## Why we cannot find the beginning

This may explain why the absolute beginning of sense is so difficult to place.

Every attempt to locate it begins from a standpoint already organized by previous achievements.

We can study development earlier and earlier.

We can study prelinguistic action.

We can study nonhuman organisms.

We can study simpler forms of discrimination and adaptation.

Those empirical questions matter.

But phenomenologically, every point we can identify is already downstream of enough organization for something to appear there.

We do not stand outside the process and watch it start.

We turn around from within it.

And every time we turn around, we are standing on something it has already made available.

## Nobody starts from zero, again

The earlier claim that nobody starts from zero first appeared as a claim about development and civilization.

Children inherit language, tools, practices, institutions, and environments already worked by others.

Adults inherit results they did not personally derive.

Civilizations preserve non-zero starting points so that later people do not need to begin again.

The present claim pushes the same structure downward.

A question does not start from zero.

A search does not start from zero.

A failure does not start from zero.

An opening does not start from zero.

Even the experience of not knowing begins from enough that the not-knowing can be definite.

The phrase therefore names something more general than cultural inheritance.

> **Every live matter begins inside conditions that are already sufficiently organized for that matter to become live.**

## A recursive architecture

The architecture now appears recursive.

What was achieved before changes what can become insufficient now.

What becomes insufficient recruits work.

What becomes enough now changes what can become insufficient later.

Sense-making therefore continually changes the conditions of its own future occurrence.

This is why learning changes what can be learned next.

Why language changes what can be asked.

Why expertise changes what becomes noticeable.

Why tools change what counts as possible.

Why institutions change what individuals may begin from.

Why a new concept can reveal a problem that could not previously be formulated.

Every achievement can alter the field of later openings.

The architecture does not merely carry answers forward.

It carries forward the ability to have different questions.

## The claim

The proposal can now be stated plainly.

> **We do not ordinarily encounter sense as something absent and then manufactured. We encounter an already organized field whose organization becomes locally insufficient. Sense-seeking operates within that insufficiency. Sense-making is the achievement by which the field is reorganized enough for continuation. What remains from that achievement becomes part of the inherited organization from which later limits, questions, and possibilities can appear.**

Or more compactly:

> **We may never experience sense itself as absent. We experience its limits.**

This claim does not settle the metaphysics of mind.

It does not establish that the current 54-form morphology is complete.

It does not tell us whether every organism realizes the same structures.

It does not answer the question of consciousness.

It does something smaller and, perhaps, more useful.

It identifies where to look.

Not for the creation of sense from nothing.

For the place where what already makes sense ceases to be enough.

And for the event by which there is suddenly somewhere new to stand.

## What this adds to Making Sense Together

The earlier architecture says:

Something is open.

Something changes.

The change becomes enough.

Something remains.

What remains changes what can happen next.

The present claim adds one more relation:

> **What is open is itself made possible by what already remains.**

Inheritance does not merely follow the event.

It helps constitute the next opening.

That makes the architecture a loop rather than a line.

And it gives the project a new leading question:

> **How does inherited intelligibility generate the conditions under which its own limits can become visible?**

That question joins several others now waiting at the edge:

- What distinguishes sense-seeking from sense-making empirically?
- When does attention mark a reopening of inherited organization?
- How quickly does a completed event become a starting condition?
- What kinds of persistence are necessary for a later insufficiency even to be noticed?
- Can different histories produce different openings in the same physical situation?
- Can we measure the moment at which a previously invisible limit becomes live?
- How does a system preserve enough continuity to reorganize without starting over?

These are adjoining questions.

They do not require the original object to be abandoned.

They appear because the object has become clearer.

There is no need to invent another beginning.

We are already inside it.
